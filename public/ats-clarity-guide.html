Clear Resume Formatting for Applicant Systems

Applicant tracking systems differ in their features and settings, so no single formatting rule guarantees that every system will interpret a resume perfectly. A practical approach is to make the document readable for people while keeping its structure straightforward. Clear section labels and consistent text help both a hurried reviewer and software that extracts document fields.

Use familiar section labels

Headings such as Experience, Education, Skills, and Projects tell a reader what kind of information follows. Creative labels can be meaningful in a portfolio, but an unfamiliar heading on a resume may make the content harder to find. Use the simplest label that accurately describes the section.

Keep the reading order obvious

Avoid layouts where columns, floating text boxes, or decorative sidebars separate a job title from its dates and description. If you use a designed template, export it and inspect the extracted text to see whether the reading order remains sensible. The file should still communicate when its visual styling is removed.

Write keywords in context

Include a skill term when it accurately describes your experience and supports the role you are pursuing. Place it in a project, responsibility, or accomplishment that shows how you used it. Repeating a phrase without context can make the document less useful to people and does not prove competence.

Verify the submitted file

Follow the employer’s requested format. Open the final PDF or document, select and copy a few lines, and check that text appears in the expected order. Confirm that links work and that the filename is professional. These simple checks improve reliability without claiming to know how a specific employer configures its system.

Frequently asked questions

Is a two-column resume always rejected?

No universal rule applies to every system. A simpler layout usually makes reading order easier to inspect, while the employer’s instructions should guide the file format.

Does an ATS score reflect hiring quality?

A score from a third-party checker is only an estimate based on that tool’s own criteria. It cannot represent every employer system or substitute for a human review.
